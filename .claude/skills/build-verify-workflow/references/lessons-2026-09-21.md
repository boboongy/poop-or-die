# Lessons: day 3 (Level 2 "Wet floor", four owner playtest rounds), why it took so long

Scope: one long session (with two context restarts): Level 2 built from a plan, then four owner playtest rounds (tissue and sink block; mop, puddle and blocking; more puddles and an easier mop; timer, mop controls and talk box). Times below are ESTIMATES (the session was not timed); the test-speed numbers were measured.
Cause classes: **Process**, **Missing context**, **Weak rule/mechanism**, **Unreliable code** (as in lessons-2026-09-19-20.md).

## Where the time went (estimates)
- Waiting for tests: well over two hours, probably about three. Full suite about 15 min at the start, 35-40 min after Level 2, about 75 min at the end, plus dozens of 3-12 minute reruns, all one after another in real time on a 12-core machine.
- About a dozen "fix the test, rerun" cycles of 3-6 minutes each (most first-run failures of new tests were test bugs).
- Four owner rounds, three of them about things a player-eye screenshot would have shown.
- Two rounds of clarifying questions plus a re-plan for "more puddles".
- About ten failed Edit calls (tab depth) and one hung shell (an empty heredoc): minutes, not hours.

## Totals
Process about 9, weak rule/mechanism 1 (but it caused several others), missing context 2, unreliable code 1 real bug (walker deadlock) plus test-side flakiness from real-time runs. **The pattern: the slow feedback loop and the unread rules were the main causes; the knowledge was mostly available.**

## What worked (keep doing)
- Test every instance (20 stalls, 10 sinks): it found the solid-block pocket and the tap and reward edge cases before the owner did.
- Failing test first for owner reports (tissue spots, mop visibility, talk box: each failed before the fix).
- Probes before code (floor rectangles, ChaseBlock, corridor widths).
- The stress test's encounter counter and self-diagnosis: it exposed a real bug and explained it in one run.
- Measuring effort with a bot before deciding a timer or a control change.
- Correcting a wrong claim openly and fixing it (blocking).
